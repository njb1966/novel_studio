## CATEGORY 1: BANNED WORDS

### Verbs — Ban or Severely Restrict

- **Delve** — the single most notorious AI word
- **Resonate / resonated** — AI's universal verb for emotional impact
- **Unleash / unlock** — AI's favorite verbs for potential or power
- **Navigate** — AI's metaphor for any difficulty
- **Foster** — AI's go-to for any nurturing relationship
- **Underscore** — AI's synonym for "emphasize"
- **Harness** — especially "harness the power of"
- **Embark** — AI's default for any beginning
- **Elevate** — AI's word for any improvement
- **Craft / crafted** — AI overuses this for anything made with care

### Adjectives — Ban or Severely Restrict

- **Palpable** — "a palpable sense of" is one of AI's most common constructions
- **Profound** — AI's default for any deep emotion or realization
- **Visceral** — AI uses this constantly while producing prose that is the opposite of visceral
- **Myriad** — AI reaches for this instead of specific numbers or descriptions
- **Pivotal** — AI marks every moment as pivotal
- **Multifaceted** — AI's word for anything complex
- **Unwavering** — AI's word for any loyalty or determination
- **Tapestry** — "a rich tapestry of" is peak AI prose
- **Robust** — AI's favorite meaningless intensifier
- **Seamless** — "a seamless blend of" appears in AI text constantly
- **Cutting-edge**

### Nouns and Noun Phrases — Ban

- **Tapestry** (listed again — it's that bad)
- **Beacon** — "a beacon of hope/light"
- **Realm** — "in the realm of"
- **Landscape** — used metaphorically ("the landscape of his grief")
- **Testament** — "a testament to"
- **Symphony** — "a symphony of"
- **Crucible** — "the crucible of war/experience"
- **Specter** — AI reaches for this whenever something haunting appears
- **Labyrinth** — AI's go-to for confusion or complexity
- **Nexus** — AI's word for any intersection

### Novel-Specific Bans

- **Ancient power** — too generic for a novel about actual ancient power; earn specificity instead
- **Primal** — AI uses this as shorthand for "old and intense"; find specific physical language
- **Something shifted / something stirred** — AI's way to indicate inner change without committing to what changed; if you use "something," name it
- **The weight of** (centuries, years, memory, grief) — AI leans on this constantly; find physical, specific ways to convey heaviness

## CATEGORY 2: BANNED SENTENCE PATTERNS

### The "Couldn't Help But" Construction
- "He couldn't help but feel..."
- "She couldn't help but notice..."
- **Ban entirely.** Describe the perception or emotion directly. Not "He couldn't help but notice the silence" — just "The silence was there, sudden, like a hand over his mouth."

### The "A Sense of [Emotion]" Construction
- "A sense of unease washed over him"
- "A sense of dread settled in his chest"
- "There was a palpable sense of anticipation"
- **Ban entirely.** Show the emotion through the body, through action, through the specific texture of the moment. Not "A sense of grief settled over him" — "His hands opened and closed on nothing."

### The "Washed Over / Swept Through / Flooded" Emotional Delivery
- "A wave of sadness washed over him"
- "Relief swept through her"
- "Memory flooded back"
- **Ban the liquid-emotion pattern.** Emotions should arrive as specific physical sensations, not as generic tides moving over a passive character.

### The "His/Her Voice Was [Adjective]" Construction
- "His voice was barely above a whisper"
- "Her voice carried a quiet intensity"
- "His voice was thick with emotion"
- **Restrict severely.** Let the words spoken and the context convey the quality of the voice. Or describe the physical mechanics: a voice that cracks, a voice that comes from too deep in the chest.

### The "Eyes That [Verb]" Construction
- "Eyes that held a thousand years of sorrow"
- "Her eyes searched his face"
- "His eyes darkened"
- **Restrict to rare, earned moments.** Characters have entire bodies. Use the whole instrument: hands, posture, the way they hold their shoulders, the rhythm of their breathing.

### The "Hung in the Air" Construction
- "The words hung in the air"
- "The silence hung between them"
- "The question hung in the air like smoke"
- **Ban entirely.** Find other ways to convey the weight of a moment — through what happens next, through what the body does, through the specific quality of the silence.

### The "It Was As If / As Though" Simile Crutch
- "It was as if the world had stopped"
- "As though something ancient had awakened"
- **Restrict to no more than once per chapter.** Similes are powerful when rare. Log each use in BIBLE.md Section G before submitting the chapter.

### The "Not X, But Y" Rhetorical Pattern
- "It wasn't fear, exactly — it was something older"
- "Not a sound, but the memory of a sound"
- "It wasn't madness. It was something else entirely."
- **Restrict to rare, genuinely necessary moments.** When every insight is delivered as "not X, but Y," the reader feels a tic rather than a mind. One per scene at most. The clarification must be genuinely earned, not decorative.

## CATEGORY 3: BANNED STRUCTURAL HABITS

### The Balanced Paragraph
AI produces paragraphs that look like perfect rectangles: three sentences, all roughly 15–20 words long, standard Subject-Verb-Object structure. **Vary paragraph length deliberately.** Some paragraphs should be one sentence. Some should be six. Follow a long, complex sentence with a short, blunt one. The prose must have burstiness — unpredictable rhythm.

### The Throat-Clearing Opening
AI almost always begins chapters and scenes with a soft, contextualizing sentence before getting to the point. **Begin chapters in the middle of the moment, not before it.** No "The morning was quiet as [PROTAGONIST] made his way to..." — start with the action, start with the body, start with the thing that matters.

### The Emotional Summary at Paragraph End
AI habitually ends paragraphs by telling the reader what to feel about what just happened. "He stood there for a long time, the weight of everything he had lost pressing down on him." **Ban this.** The final sentence of a paragraph should advance the scene, not summarize the emotion. Trust the reader to feel what you have shown them.

### The Rhythmic Triplet
AI defaults to lists of three in emotional or descriptive passages: "The grief, the rage, the unbearable loneliness." **Restrict to rare, deliberate use.** Vary your lists — sometimes two items, sometimes four, sometimes one item that carries the full weight alone.

## PRODUCTION RULES

**Before submitting any beat or chapter:**
1. Grep the prose for every word in Category 1. Remove or replace.
2. Read every sentence aloud for Category 2 patterns.
3. Count em dashes. Justify each one or cut it.
4. Count "as if / as though" uses. Maximum one per chapter. Log in BIBLE.md Section G.
5. Check paragraph lengths. If three consecutive paragraphs are the same length, break one.
6. Read the final paragraph of every scene. If it summarizes the emotion, cut it or replace it with action.
